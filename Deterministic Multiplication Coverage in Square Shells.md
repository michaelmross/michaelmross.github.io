# Deterministic Multiplication Coverage in Square Shells

## 1. The basic observation

Consider the square shell

$$
n^2 < m < (n+1)^2.
$$

There are exactly $n$ odd integers in this interval.

If an odd $m$ in the shell is composite, write $m=ab$ with $a\le b$. Since

$$
n < \sqrt m < n+1,
$$

we necessarily have

$$
a\le n<b.
$$

Thus every odd composite in the shell is generated by multiplication from an odd factor $a\le n$. Conversely, any odd integer in the shell not generated by such a multiplication row must be prime.

This gives an exact deterministic reformulation of prime recurrence between consecutive squares:

> **Prime recurrence is equivalent to failure of complete multiplication coverage of the $n$ odd positions in the square shell.**

No distributional or probabilistic assertion is involved.

---

## 2. Multiplication rows

For each odd $a$, $3\le a\le n$, define the row

$$
R_a(n)=
\{m:n^2<m<(n+1)^2,\ m\text{ odd},\ a\mid m\}.
$$

Equivalently, writing

$$
m=n^2+k,\qquad 1\le k\le2n,
$$

the row covers offsets satisfying

$$
k\equiv -n^2\pmod a.
$$

If

$$
n=qa+r,\qquad 0\le r<a,
$$

then this becomes

$$
k\equiv-r^2\pmod a.
$$

The rows can therefore be grouped naturally into quotient bands

$$
q=\left\lfloor\frac na\right\rfloor,
$$

or

$$
\frac{n}{q+1}<a\le\frac nq.
$$

This is a rigid square-anchored covering system rather than an arbitrary collection of residue classes.

---

## 3. Composite rows are redundant

If $a$ is composite and $d\mid a$ with $1<d<a$, then

$$
R_a(n)\subseteq R_d(n).
$$

Hence a composite row cannot cover a position that has not already been covered by a smaller factor.

One may therefore process the odd row labels in increasing order and retain only those that contribute genuinely irreducible multiplication information. These surviving labels are precisely the odd primes, but they can be generated by multiplication alone rather than supplied by a primality oracle.

Call these the **irreducible rows**.

---

## 4. Raw coverage and collision excess

Let $E_n$ be the total number of multiplication hits from the rows, counted with multiplicity, and let $C_n$ be the set of distinct covered odd positions.

Then

$$
|C_n|=E_n-X_n,
$$

where

$$
X_n=E_n-|C_n|
$$

is the collision or redundancy excess.

Since there are $n$ candidate positions, the number of uncovered positions is

$$
D_n=n-|C_n|.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
D_n=n-E_n+X_n.
}
$$

Equivalently, defining

$$
\text{edge surplus}=E_n-n,
$$

we obtain the exact identity

$$
\boxed{
D_n=
\text{collision excess}
-
\text{edge surplus}.
}
$$

Complete coverage would require the multiplication rows to use all their excess capacity without losing too much of it to compulsory intersections.

The computations show that this is emphatically not what happens: raw multiplication capacity can greatly exceed $n$, while enormous overlap leaves many positions uncovered.

---

## 5. Multiplicative depth

For each odd shell position $m$, define

$$
w(m)=
\#\{a\le n:
a\text{ is an irreducible odd row and }a\mid m\}.
$$

Thus $w(m)$ is the number of distinct irreducible multiplication rows passing through $m$.

Then

- $w(m)=0$ means the position is uncovered;
- $w(m)=1$ means it is covered uniquely;
- $w(m)\ge2$ measures multiplicative collision depth.

The exact number of uncovered positions is

$$
D_n=\#\{m:w(m)=0\}.
$$

---

## 6. The Bonferroni hierarchy

Define the intersection sums

$$
S_j(n)=
\sum_m {w(m)\choose j}.
$$

Thus:

- $S_1$ counts all irreducible-row hits;
- $S_2$ counts pairwise intersections;
- $S_3$ counts triple intersections;
- and so on.

For odd $K$, define

$$
U_K
=
S_1-S_2+S_3-\cdots+S_K.
$$

By the Bonferroni inequalities,

$$
|C_n|\le U_K.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
D_n\ge n-U_K.
}
$$

In particular,

$$
\boxed{
U_K<n
}
$$

is a deterministic certificate that complete multiplication coverage is impossible.

This is the main obstruction found so far.

It does **not** yet prove that complete coverage is impossible for every $n$, because we have not proved that some suitable $U_K<n$ universally. But whenever the inequality holds, failure of complete coverage follows rigorously from finite multiplication overlap alone.

---

## 7. Exact truncation error

The Bonferroni approximation has an especially useful exact error formula. For odd $K$,

$$
U_K-|C_n|
=
\sum_{w\ge K+1}
N_w{w-1\choose K},
$$

where

$$
N_w=\#\{m:w(m)=w\}.
$$

Hence

$$
D_n-(n-U_K)
=
\sum_{w\ge K+1}
N_w{w-1\choose K}.
$$

So the only error in the order-$K$ calculation comes from positions of multiplicative depth greater than $K$.

If no position has depth exceeding $K$, then inclusion-exclusion terminates and

$$
\boxed{
U_K=|C_n|
}
$$

exactly.

---

## 8. Computation through $n=10^4$

The first computation examined every shell

$$
2\le n\le10000.
$$

For all $9999$ shells,

$$
\boxed{
U_5<n.
}
$$

Thus fifth-order multiplicative overlap alone certifies at least one uncovered position in every tested shell.

By contrast, order $3$ first fails as a certificate at

$$
n=696,
$$

and fails for most larger shells in this range.

Orders $7$ and $9$ also certify every shell through $10^4$.

At $n=10000$:

$$
D_{10000}=1081,
$$

while fifth order already guarantees

$$
1043
$$

holes.

Thus fivefold intersection data accounts for almost all of the exact deficit.

---

## 9. Larger examples

### $n=10^6$

The computation gives

$$
D_n=72413.
$$

The Bonferroni hierarchy is:

$$
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r}
K & U_K & n-U_K & \text{slack}\\
\hline
5 & 970320 & 29680 & 42733\\
7 & 927792 & 72208 & 205\\
9 & 927587 & 72413 & 0
\end{array}
$$

Thus order $9$ is exact.

The entire million-position covering problem is determined by intersections involving at most nine irreducible rows.

---

### $n=10^7$

Here

$$
D_n=620979.
$$

The hierarchy includes

$$
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r}
K & U_K & n-U_K & \text{slack}\\
\hline
7 & 9387220 & 612780 & 8199\\
9 & 9379028 & 620972 & 7\\
11 & 9379021 & 620979 & 0
\end{array}
$$

So order $11$ is exact.

The slack $7$ at order $9$ implies that only a tiny number of very high-depth positions prevent order $9$ from already being exact.

---

## 10. The odd primorial boundary

Define

$$
Q_r
=
3\cdot5\cdot7\cdots p_{r+1},
$$

the product of the first $r$ odd primes.

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{
Q_r=\frac{p_{r+1}\#}{2}.
}
$$

This is the smallest odd integer having $r$ distinct irreducible factors.

Therefore no shell integer below $Q_r$ can have multiplicative depth $r$.

This produces a deterministic upper bound on the maximum possible depth.

If

$$
(n+1)^2<Q_{K+1},
$$

then no shell position can have depth $K+1$, and an odd Bonferroni order $K$ is necessarily exact.

---

## 11. The primorial staircase

The first shell capable of containing an $r$-fold intersection is the shell containing $Q_r$:

$$
n_r=
\left\lfloor\sqrt{Q_r}\right\rfloor.
$$

For example,

$$
Q_{12}
=
\frac{41\#}{2}
=
152125131763605,
$$

and

$$
\left\lfloor\sqrt{Q_{12}}\right\rfloor
=
12333901.
$$

At

$$
n=12333901,
$$

the shell contains $Q_{12}$ itself, giving a guaranteed depth-$12$ position.

The computation showed:

$$
U_{11}-U_{13}=1,
$$

with

$$
\text{slack}_{11}=1,
$$

and $U_{13}$ exact.

Thus the first forced depth-$12$ witness causes exactly the predicted transition in the Bonferroni hierarchy.

---

## 12. The $Q_{13}$ transition

Similarly,

$$
Q_{13}
=
\frac{43\#}{2}
=
6541380665835015,
$$

and

$$
\left\lfloor\sqrt{Q_{13}}\right\rfloor
=
80878802.
$$

For the shell $n=80878802$, the depth histogram included

$$
N_{13}=1.
$$

The unique guaranteed depth-$13$ witness is $Q_{13}$ itself.

The shell had

$$
D_n=4440512.
$$

The Bonferroni hierarchy gave approximately:

$$
\begin{array}{c|r|r}
K & n-U_K & \text{slack}\\
\hline
7 & 4276025 & 164487\\
9 & 4440037 & 475\\
11 & 4440500 & 12\\
13 & 4440512 & 0
\end{array}
$$

Thus order $7$ alone already proves more than $4.27$ million uncovered positions, while order $13$ gives the exact answer.

The adjacent shell $n=80878803$ has maximum depth only $11$, illustrating that high multiplicative depth appears locally rather than monotonically.

---

## 13. Growth of the required exact depth

Since

$$
Q_r=\prod_{j=2}^{r+1}p_j,
$$

standard growth of the primorial gives approximately

$$
\log Q_r\sim r\log r.
$$

The condition

$$
Q_r\lesssim n^2
$$

therefore suggests

$$
r\sim
\frac{2\log n}{\log\log n}.
$$

Thus an exact inclusion-exclusion calculation requires only about

$$
\boxed{
O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)
}
$$

levels of multiplicative intersection.

This is striking because the shell has $n$ candidate positions and very many available multiplication rows, yet its exact coverage is governed by a very shallow local overlap hierarchy.

---

## 14. What has been achieved

The multiplication formulation gives several exact deterministic facts.

1. Every odd composite in $(n^2,(n+1)^2)$ is generated by an odd multiplication row $a\le n$.

2. Composite rows are redundant; only irreducible rows matter.

3. Prime recurrence is exactly equivalent to failure of complete multiplication coverage.

4. The failure of coverage can be quantified by compulsory multiplicative collisions.

5. For every odd $K$,

   $$
   U_K<n
   $$

   is a rigorous deterministic obstruction to complete coverage.

6. The truncation error of $U_K$ is determined exactly by positions of multiplicative depth greater than $K$.

7. Odd primorials $Q_r=p_{r+1}\#/2$ determine the absolute thresholds at which new multiplicative depths first become possible.

8. The exact inclusion-exclusion depth grows only very slowly, roughly as $2\log n/\log\log n$.

---

## 15. What has *not* been proved

The crucial unresolved step is universal.

We have not proved that for every $n$ there exists an odd $K$ for which

$$
U_K<n
$$

without already evaluating the shell completely.

Such a result would establish that multiplication coverage can never be complete and would therefore prove that every interval

$$
(n^2,(n+1)^2)
$$

contains a prime.

In other words:

> **We have identified a deterministic obstruction to complete coverage, but have not yet proved that the obstruction is unavoidable for every square shell.**

The present evidence strongly suggests that compulsory multiplicative overlap is the mechanism to study.

---

## 16. The next mathematical question

The most concrete next target is to seek an inequality of the form

$$
S_1-S_2+S_3-\cdots+S_K<n
$$

for some controlled odd $K$.

A particularly attractive possibility would be a fixed low order over a large range, for example

$$
U_7<n,
$$

or more generally a slowly growing order

$$
K=O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right).
$$

The central question is therefore no longer merely

> “Is there a prime between consecutive squares?”

but rather:

> **Can the square-anchored multiplication rows ever avoid enough compulsory collisions to cover all $n$ odd positions?**

That is the deterministic coverage problem exposed by the multiplication boundaries.
